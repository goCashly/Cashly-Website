Who Uses a Private Refinance

A refinance often solves more than one problem at once.

One structured move can resolve debt pressure, catch up on arrears, or fund renovations, when the math works better than stacking more debt.

Debt consolidation

Roll high-interest credit cards or loans into one mortgage payment secured against your home equity.

Tax arrears

CRA arrears can block bank renewals. A private refinance can clear arrears using your equity position.

Renovations

Fund a larger renovation project with one structured amount instead of revolving credit.

Cash-flow reset

Create short-term breathing room while you work toward a cleaner long-term solution.

Why Private, Not Bank

Why the bank said no, and why that isn't the whole story.

A decline feels personal. Usually it's a rule you didn't happen to fit. Here's what actually stops most bank refinance applications.

01 Banks lend on income and credit, not equity

Lender qualification rules require banks to assess declared income, credit history, and the stress test. Strong equity doesn't offset a thin credit file.

02 A credit event doesn't erase your equity

Consumer proposals, bankruptcy discharges, collections, and low scores disqualify most bank applications outright. Private lenders look at the property first.

03 Self-employed write-offs work against you

Business owners who legitimately minimize taxable income often show a return that understates real cash flow. Banks qualify on the declared number.

04 Some timelines can't wait on bank underwriting

Tax arrears deadlines or maturing terms don't always leave room for a multi-week bank process.

Private Refinance FAQ

Questions about private mortgage refinancing in Ontario.

What is a private mortgage refinance?

A private refinance replaces your existing mortgage with a new one from a private lender, based primarily on your property's equity rather than income or credit.

How is a private refinance different from a second mortgage?

A refinance replaces your existing first mortgage with a new one. A second mortgage sits behind your current first mortgage as a separate loan. Which makes more sense depends on your current mortgage's rate, term, and any prepayment penalty.

What does a private refinance typically cost?

Costs usually include a lender fee, broker fee, legal registration, and an appraisal. Every fee that applies to your file is disclosed in writing before you commit to anything.

Can I refinance with bad credit or a consumer proposal?

Private refinance approval is weighted toward your property's equity rather than a bank-style credit and income review, so a consumer proposal or credit event doesn't automatically rule it out.

When does a refinance make more sense than a HELOC?

A refinance tends to fit better when you need one lump sum to solve a specific problem. A HELOC fits better when you want ongoing, staged access to equity instead.
