Private HELOC Ontario

Draw on Your Home Equity Only When You Need It.

A private HELOC gives Ontario homeowners revolving access to home equity, without refinancing your entire first mortgage or taking one lump sum you don't need yet.

Who Uses a Private HELOC

A HELOC fits best when you don't need every dollar at once.

Revolving equity access tends to work well for projects and expenses that happen in stages rather than all at once.

Staged renovations

Draw funds as each phase of a renovation project is completed instead of borrowing the full cost upfront.

Business cash flow

Keep a flexible equity cushion available for a business that has uneven or seasonal cash flow.

Recurring large expenses

Tuition, medical costs, or other expenses that come up repeatedly rather than as a single event.

Flexible, ongoing access

Keep the line open and available rather than re-applying every time a new need comes up.

Why a HELOC

What makes a HELOC different from a lump-sum refinance.

A HELOC is a structure decision as much as a lending decision. Here's what we weigh before recommending one.

01 Revolving access, not a lump sum

You draw what you need, repay it, and can draw again, rather than receiving one fixed amount at closing.

02 Sits behind your existing mortgage

A private HELOC is typically registered behind your current first mortgage and doesn't require refinancing it.

03 Approval weighted toward equity

Like other private lending, a HELOC is assessed primarily on your property's equity position rather than income documentation.

04 Best for staged or ongoing needs

If you need everything at once, a refinance or second mortgage is usually the simpler structure instead.

Private HELOC FAQ

Questions about private HELOCs in Ontario.

What is a private HELOC?

A private HELOC is a revolving home equity line of credit funded by a private lender rather than a bank. It lets you draw, repay, and redraw funds against your home's equity as needed, instead of receiving one lump sum.

How is a HELOC different from refinancing?

A refinance replaces your existing mortgage and typically pays out as a single lump sum. A HELOC is usually a separate line registered behind your current first mortgage, giving you ongoing access instead of one upfront amount.

Can I get a private HELOC with bad credit or if I'm self-employed?

Private HELOC approval is weighted more heavily toward your property's equity than your credit score or traditional income documentation, so it's often accessible to self-employed homeowners and borrowers with credit challenges.

What can I use a HELOC for?

Common uses include staged renovations, business cash flow, tuition, debt consolidation, and other recurring or large expenses where you don't want to borrow the full amount upfront.

Does a HELOC affect my existing first mortgage?

A private HELOC is typically registered behind your existing first mortgage and doesn't require you to refinance or disturb your current mortgage terms.

Is a HELOC the same as a second mortgage?

They're related but structured differently. A second mortgage is usually a one-time lump sum repaid on a fixed schedule. A HELOC is revolving credit registered in a similar position behind your first mortgage, letting you draw, repay, and redraw rather than receiving one fixed amount.

How much equity do I need for a private HELOC?

Equity requirements vary by lender and property, and private lenders generally look for meaningful equity remaining after the HELOC is added. Tell us your property value and current mortgage balance and we'll let you know what's realistic for your file.

Can I get a private HELOC after a consumer proposal or bankruptcy?

Often, yes. Because private HELOC approval is based primarily on home equity rather than a bank-style credit review, a past consumer proposal or discharged bankruptcy doesn't automatically rule you out the way it typically would at a bank.
